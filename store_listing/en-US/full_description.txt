WordWaverise is an English dictionary with the practice built around it. The difference from a translator is one thing: a word is broken down into its senses, and you study the sense you actually ran into.

📖 <b>The entry</b><br>
• Senses grouped by part of speech: Russian translation, an English explanation, and examples with translations.<br>
• CEFR level (A1–C2) and register labels: informal, formal, literary, slang, dated.<br>
• Countability for nouns where it changes the meaning: paper the material is uncountable, paper the document is not.<br>
• Transcription and audio per part of speech — and per sense when they sound different. "lead" is both /liːd/ and /led/; bass, bow, live, record and present work the same way.<br>
• Synonyms, antonyms, word forms and source links under every sense.<br>
• A quick version of the entry arrives in seconds; the full one replaces it on its own.

🔍 <b>Search</b><br>
• Suggestions as you type, each with its translation and part of speech instead of a bare list of spellings.<br>
• Typos are corrected (recieve → receive), but a word that really exists is never swapped for a lookalike.<br>
• Russian to English: type «решать» and get resolve, settle and decide with an explanation of how they differ.<br>
• Sentence breakdown: paste a phrase, tap a word, and the app gives you its dictionary form, what it means right there, and a translation of the whole sentence.

🗂 <b>Your vocabulary</b><br>
• You save a sense, not a spelling. "Resolve" as "to settle" and "resolve" as "to break down" are two separate entries, each with its own translation, example, pronunciation and review schedule.<br>
• Folders: a word can sit in as many as you like, and folders themselves nest into groups — lessons into a module, topics into a course.<br>
• Search and sorting for both words and folders.<br>
• Saved words and folders open without a connection.

🎴 <b>Flashcards</b><br>
• The order is deliberate: word → the English definition of the sense you picked → the translation, one tap further. A card that opens with the translation answers its own question.<br>
• Transcription and audio come from the part of speech you chose, so the card for the metal doesn't play the verb.<br>
• Spaced repetition: Know / Hard / Forgot, today's queue, and statistics.<br>
• Build a deck from a folder in one tap. Card text can be rewritten, and your edit survives the refresh from the dictionary.

✏️ <b>Exercises</b><br>
Nine types: choose the meaning, choose the word, translate both ways, fill the blank in a real sentence, pick the word that fits the context, collocations, spelling, and listening — a question with nothing on screen but the audio.<br>
A session is drawn from a folder and shuffled across types, so ten questions of the same shape never come in a row. Wrong options are picked to be close in meaning: guessing without knowing the word doesn't work. Two swapped letters count as "almost", not as a miss.

👩‍🏫 <b>Classes</b><br>
• A teacher creates a group, invites students with a link or an eight-character code, and shares folders and assignments with a goal and a deadline.<br>
• Students copy nothing: the teacher's folder opens as their own, while cards and progress stay personal.<br>
• Both sides see how far an assignment has got.

🔗 <b>Shared folders</b><br>
Any folder can be shared by link, senses included, not just a list of spellings. The recipient gets a copy, and the words they already had stay exactly where they were.

⚙️ <b>Also</b><br>
Sign in with Google or email. Light and dark themes. Account deletion from inside the app. Any text can be reported — the button sits next to every sense.

Entry texts and some exercises are written by a language model from dictionary sources; the sources are linked under each sense.
