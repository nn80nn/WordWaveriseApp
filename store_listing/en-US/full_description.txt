WordWaverise is an English dictionary with the practice built around it. The difference from a translator is one thing: a word is broken down into its senses, and you study the sense you actually ran into.

THE ENTRY

• Senses grouped by part of speech: Russian translation, an English explanation, and examples with translations.
• CEFR level (A1–C2) and register labels: informal, formal, literary, slang, dated.
• Countability for nouns where it changes the meaning: paper the material is uncountable, paper the document is not.
• Transcription and audio per part of speech — and per sense when they sound different. "lead" is both /liːd/ and /led/; bass, bow, live, record and present work the same way.
• Synonyms, antonyms, word forms and source links under every sense.
• A quick version of the entry arrives in seconds; the full one replaces it on its own.

SEARCH

• Suggestions as you type, each with its translation and part of speech instead of a bare list of spellings.
• Typos are corrected (recieve → receive), but a word that really exists is never swapped for a lookalike.
• Russian to English: type "решать" and get resolve, settle and decide with an explanation of how they differ.
• Sentence breakdown: paste a phrase, tap a word, and the app gives you its dictionary form, what it means right there, and a translation of the whole sentence.

YOUR VOCABULARY

• You save a sense, not a spelling. "Resolve" as "to settle" and "resolve" as "to break down" are two separate entries, each with its own translation, example, pronunciation and review schedule.
• Folders: a word can sit in as many as you like, and folders themselves nest into groups — lessons into a module, topics into a course.
• Search and sorting for both words and folders.
• Saved words and folders open without a connection.

FLASHCARDS

• The order is deliberate: word → the English definition of the sense you picked → the translation, one tap further. A card that opens with the translation answers its own question.
• Transcription and audio come from the part of speech you chose, so the card for the metal doesn't play the verb.
• Spaced repetition: Know / Hard / Forgot, today's queue, and statistics.
• Build a deck from a folder in one tap. Card text can be rewritten, and your edit survives the refresh from the dictionary.

EXERCISES

Nine types: choose the meaning, choose the word, translate both ways, fill the blank in a real sentence, pick the word that fits the context, collocations, spelling, and listening — a question with nothing on screen but the audio.

A session is drawn from a folder and shuffled across types, so ten questions of the same shape never come in a row. Wrong options are picked to be close in meaning: guessing without knowing the word doesn't work. Two swapped letters count as "almost", not as a miss.

CLASSES

• A teacher creates a group, invites students with a link or an eight-character code, and shares folders and assignments with a goal and a deadline.
• Students copy nothing: the teacher's folder opens as their own, while cards and progress stay personal.
• Both sides see how far an assignment has got.

SHARED FOLDERS

Any folder can be shared by link, senses included, not just a list of spellings. The recipient gets a copy, and the words they already had stay exactly where they were.

ALSO

Sign in with Google or email. Light and dark themes. Account deletion from inside the app. Any text can be reported — the button sits next to every sense.

Entry texts and some exercises are written by a language model from dictionary sources; the sources are linked under each sense.
